# Character performance and UGC video recipes

## UGC product ad

Required inputs: person/creator authority, product authority, verified benefits, environment, platform, duration, CTA, and whether dialogue/audio must be generated.

1. Create a credible vertical hero still that combines person and product with natural grip/use, legible product, plausible phone-camera framing, and practical light.
2. Approve face, hands, product geometry/label, and environment before video.
3. Write short spoken copy as hook -> personal context/problem -> demonstration -> result/proof -> CTA. Fit the word count to the supported duration and natural delivery.
4. Separate visible action from spoken line in each timed beat.
5. Use restrained handheld behavior, eye-line to camera, natural micro-gestures, and one continuous performance unless cuts are explicitly planned.
6. Review lip/audio sync, claim accuracy, product handling, identity, hands, and whether the result feels intentionally native rather than merely low quality.

Do not research or invent benefits unless a trusted source is available and in scope. Mark unverified claims for user approval.

## Lifestyle try-on to motion

Approve the still fit/use proof first. Give the model one subtle performance: weight shift, hand adjustment, turn, application, or product demonstration. Preserve garment/product detail and avoid poses that hide the evidence being shown.

## Cartoon conversion plus motion transfer

1. Convert the subject to the requested stylized material/proportions while preserving identity markers and outfit.
2. Approve the stylized character.
3. Inspect whether the selected video mode accepts a motion/video reference. Assign the character still to identity/style and the video to movement only.
4. State contact, foot placement, limb count, and camera preservation requirements.

If motion-reference input is unavailable, offer a manually directed dance/action prompt and disclose the loss of exact choreography.

## Talking character, baby, or animal vlogger

Create and approve the character image first. Keep the script short and assign language, voice age/tone, eye-line, expression, head motion, hand/prop behavior, environment ambience, and camera mode. Prefer one uninterrupted performance. If lipsync or native speech is unsupported, produce the visual performance plus a separate script/audio plan.

## Award or staged ceremony

Create a role manifest before prompting:

- winner identity and starting location;
- host identity and stage role;
- award/prop owner;
- exact spoken name and exact display copy;
- audience/environment authority.

Use a generation timeline inside the video prompt: suspenseful announcement -> reaction -> movement to stage -> handoff -> hero hold. Give every event a consecutive time window and visible end state as defined in `generation-timeline-and-storyboard.md`. Preserve image-slot order and mention roles by slot. Review role swaps, spelling, prop continuity, face drift, crowd artifacts, and audio/dialogue timing.

## Freeze-time effect

Design the audio-visual arc as normal motion -> trigger -> frozen world -> moving subject proof -> second trigger -> resumed consequence. Choose a scene with readable suspended elements and a clear moving subject. During the frozen interval, prohibit background motion; use silence or a deliberately reduced sound bed. Keep the camera path simple enough that frozen geometry remains coherent.

## Anthropomorphic creator performance

Define the animal/character anatomy, clothing, handheld prop, environment, performance attitude, and language. Establish the source still before animation. Keep human-like behavior physically readable without randomly switching between quadruped and biped anatomy. Treat novelty as the hook, but still require a clear beat and final reaction.

## Identity and consent boundary

Use supplied identity references only for the requested production. Do not imply generated scenes are authentic recordings. For public figures, minors, or sensitive impersonation contexts, follow active policy and avoid deceptive or harmful framing.
