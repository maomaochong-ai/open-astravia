# Production planning

## Convert the brief into artifacts

Capture these decisions in the project objective and deliverables:

- business or story goal;
- audience and desired response;
- channels, ratios, durations, resolutions, and safe areas;
- message hierarchy, offer, call to action, or narrative beats;
- brand, character, product, legal, and content constraints;
- supplied references and their roles;
- required masters, variants, shots, and exports;
- approval gates and observable acceptance criteria.

## Build a stage graph

Use explicit node purposes to represent:

1. Brief and shared direction.
2. Concept candidates.
3. Selected master/reference.
4. Dependent stills or keyframes.
5. Motion proof or final shots.
6. Timeline/order.
7. Deliverable outputs.

Dependencies should explain why an asset exists. Keep independent branches parallel and dependent work downstream. A failed or rejected upstream master makes downstream derivatives stale even if they were technically generated.

## Define asset authority

For each recurring subject, product, character, or visual system, identify one approved authority asset or ledger. Downstream nodes reference it rather than inventing their own version. When the authority changes, review affected derivatives before rerunning them.

## Plan budgets by uncertainty

- High creative uncertainty: more cheap concepts, fewer finished assets.
- Approved direction but uncertain motion: one short proof shot.
- Stable master and requirements: parallel final derivatives.
- Hard-to-preserve identity/product: validate the riskiest shot before easy shots.

Prefer resumable stages. A user should be able to stop after concept approval and continue without reconstructing decisions from chat.
