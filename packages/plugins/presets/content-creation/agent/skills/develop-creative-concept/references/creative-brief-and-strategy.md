# Creative brief and strategy

## Normalize the request

Capture only decisions that change the work:

- objective and desired audience response;
- audience situation, existing belief, friction, and motivation;
- offer, story truth, product proof, or subject value;
- publishing surfaces and viewing context;
- required artifacts, formats, durations, and safe areas;
- brand, identity, legal, accessibility, and factual constraints;
- supplied references and the role of each;
- success criteria observable in the finished artifact.

Separate facts, assumptions, preferences, and open decisions. Ask only when an answer changes the central idea, cost, or hard production constraints.

## Find the strategic tension

Write one sentence for each:

- The audience currently thinks/does ...
- The content should make them think/do ...
- The credible bridge is ...
- The visual proof is ...

The concept should dramatize this change rather than merely decorate the product or topic.

## Define a creative contract

Record:

- one communication promise;
- one primary emotion;
- one recurring visual motif;
- one anchor object, character, or product truth;
- one moment of change;
- one final image;
- what must never drift.

This contract becomes the source for image prompts, shot plans, continuity ledgers, and quality review.
