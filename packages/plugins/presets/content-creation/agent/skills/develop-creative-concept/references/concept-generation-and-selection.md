# Concept generation and selection

## Generate territories, not cosmetic variants

Each territory needs a different answer to at least two of these:

- audience insight;
- narrative mechanism;
- visual metaphor or governing rule;
- emotional arc;
- proof or demonstration;
- camera/viewer relationship;
- format behavior across the campaign.

A palette swap is not a new concept. Give every territory a short name, a one-sentence promise, the visual mechanism, an example hero image or scene, extensibility notes, and its largest production risk.

## Useful concept mechanisms

- Demonstration: physical consequence proves the benefit.
- Contrast: before/after, two worlds, expectation/reality.
- Ritual: preparation and irreversible state changes create anticipation.
- Transformation: one controlled visual rule changes the subject or environment.
- Point of view: the camera adopts the product, character, witness, or environment.
- Recurring motif: one shape, reflection, sound, color, or object carries the story.
- Scale play: miniature/oversized relationships make a familiar product newly readable.
- System: a master visual grammar generates a family of coherent assets.

## Select with evidence

Score or compare:

- strategic fit;
- immediate readability;
- specificity and ownability;
- visual and narrative potential;
- ability to survive all required formats;
- reference/identity risk;
- capability fit and production cost;
- room for useful variants.

Do not average away a fatal flaw. Explain why the recommended territory wins and what must be validated by the first cheap candidate or proof shot.
