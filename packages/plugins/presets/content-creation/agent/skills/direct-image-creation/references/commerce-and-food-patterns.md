# Commerce, product, food, and beverage patterns

## Product authority

Create a product ledger before variants: silhouette, dimensions/proportions, label/logo placement, material, color, controls/seams, and must-readable copy. Use the cleanest product reference as authority.

## Product hero families

- Clean catalog: readable silhouette, neutral surface, controlled contact shadow, minimal props.
- Luxury material study: restricted palette, one key source, tactile surface, haze/condensation only when physically justified.
- Lifestyle proof: product in credible use, natural interaction, environment supports audience identity.
- Benefit demonstration: action -> physical consequence -> visible proof.
- Scale play: miniature workers or oversized product, but geometry and label remain stable.
- Surreal material transformation: inflate, soften, fold, or morph while preserving recognizability.
- Ingredient freeze: product label clear, ingredients distributed without overlap, liquid/splash physics readable.

## Food image contract

Specify dish state, plating, moisture/steam, garnish, surface, props, camera, warm/cool source, and freshness cues. Food color must remain appetizing and physically plausible. Avoid over-propping and duplicate ingredients.

Useful patterns:

- hero poster: one dish, clear plate edge, tactile surface, controlled steam;
- ingredient orbit: frozen action with each ingredient readable;
- naturalist specimen: whole, cross-section, detail, and concise labels;
- food map: geographic structure plus local dish illustrations;
- origin story: raw ingredient -> craft/process -> finished product.

## Campaign board

Use a coherent panel system: lifestyle, hero product, detail, lineup or variants. Match label geometry and light temperature across panels. Differentiate variants by one declared attribute.

## Quality gate

Reject changed package geometry, unreadable labels, impossible reflections, floating contact, random condensation, cold/gray food, repetitive ingredients, fake chart/map claims, or props that compete with the product.
