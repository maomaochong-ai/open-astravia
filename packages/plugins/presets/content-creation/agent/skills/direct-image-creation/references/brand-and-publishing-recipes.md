# Brand and publishing recipes

## Logo exploration

Required inputs: brand name, industry, audience, desired impression, and any prohibited symbols or colors. Infer a reversible style direction only when the user has not supplied one.

1. Translate strategy into 2-3 distinct mark hypotheses: word/letter mark, geometric or negative-space mark, and symbol-plus-type system.
2. Generate each hypothesis as a flat isolated mark. Keep mockups, gradients, bevels, texture, and scenery out of the selection stage.
3. Review exact spelling, silhouette at small size, monochrome survival, distinctiveness, balance, and whether the concept communicates without explanation.
4. Select one mark before producing dark/light, horizontal, icon-only, favicon, or application variants.
5. Treat raster output as concept art. Do not call it production vector artwork unless a vectorization and geometric cleanup step actually exists.

For a quick single-logo request, produce one focused concept plus spelling and small-size checks. For a branding package, use `$create-content-campaign` and make selection an explicit gate.

## Brand kit and design guide

Build one coherent system rather than parallel unrelated moodboards:

1. Establish the selected logo or a temporary identity premise as authority.
2. Define palette roles: primary, secondary, accent, light neutral, dark neutral. Record contrast intent separately from generated swatch labels.
3. Define typography roles: display, heading, body, caption, numeric/data when relevant. Recommend real font families in text; generated typography boards are visual previews, not evidence that a font exists.
4. Define spacing, corner, border, illustration/photo, icon, and motion principles when the requested system includes product UI.
5. Produce application proofs whose job is to reveal system behavior, such as stationery, package, social post, or UI components.
6. Finish with do/don't rules and unresolved production work. Never derive exact hex codes by trusting generated labels alone; verify or set them in structured text.

## Paid ad creative

Required inputs: offer/product, audience, campaign objective, claim constraints, channel, and CTA.

1. Keep offer, audience, brand, and format fixed.
2. Create 3-4 hypotheses that change one persuasion mechanism: problem/solution, benefit proof, product beauty, social proof, or lifestyle identity.
3. Pair each visual hypothesis with a short headline, support line, CTA, and intended text-safe region.
4. Select on message comprehension, claim support, stopping power, product fidelity, and format extensibility.
5. Build platform layouts from the selected direction. Recompose hierarchy for each surface instead of blind cropping.

Do not fabricate usage statistics, endorsements, prices, reviews, certifications, or product benefits. Put unverified copy in the project as placeholder/awaiting approval.

## Brochure or multi-page publishing

1. Collect final copy, page/panel count, fold/trim format, print or screen destination, brand assets, and contact/QR data.
2. Create a content architecture before visuals: cover promise, inner narrative and evidence, final CTA/contact.
3. Give every page or panel a job and shared grid. Generate pages independently only after the master grid, palette, type hierarchy, and image treatment are fixed.
4. Use explicit placeholder blocks when real copy is absent. Never ask an image model to invent business facts.
5. Review panel order, fold boundaries, bleed/safe areas, copy completeness, and cross-page consistency. Generated page images are concepts until rebuilt in an editable layout tool.

## Blog and editorial header

Derive one non-cliche visual metaphor from the article thesis. Specify subject, focal placement, palette, lens/medium, and a protected title region. Deliver the image plus one-sentence alt text and title placement guidance. Do not render the full article title into the image unless exact text is required and supported.

## Instagram and RedNote covers

1. Define the two-second visual message, focal subject, and platform-safe region.
2. Choose a native ratio and mobile-scale hierarchy.
3. For Instagram, keep one focal idea and pair it with hook, body, CTA, and a restrained hashtag set only when copy is requested.
4. For RedNote-style covers, favor clear lifestyle evidence and strong topic hierarchy; do not reduce the style to generic pastel filters or decorative stickers.
5. If overlay copy matters, generate the visual with protected space and validate exact text separately.

## YouTube thumbnail

1. Convert the title into one visible tension: before/after, conflict, surprising object, outcome, or unanswered question.
2. Use at most two primary focal elements and 3-5 words of complementary overlay copy.
3. Plan subject and copy on opposing thirds with high luminance/shape contrast.
4. Review at feed thumbnail size, not only full resolution. Reject unreadable text, weak facial/subject state, misleading imagery, or duplicated title meaning.
5. Create A/B variants only when requested or when a campaign experiment is being built; each variant changes one hypothesis.

## Object-built text and decorative lettering

For keycaps, signs, packaging, or other literal text objects, record the exact string and expected line breaks. Choose a text-capable image mode, keep the background and object grammar simple, and inspect every character. A visually attractive misspelling is a failed output.
