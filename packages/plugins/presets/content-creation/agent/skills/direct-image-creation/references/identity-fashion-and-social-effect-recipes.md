# Identity, fashion, and social-effect recipes

## Identity authority

Before any pack or transformation, inspect the source only for production properties: pose/view, crop, expression, occlusion, light direction, and image quality. Do not replace visual identity with a speculative demographic description.

Record immutable facial structure, hairline/style, skin tone, body proportions, distinguishing marks, and wardrobe/product details that must persist. Use the source image as authority and change one axis per branch.

## Professional or lifestyle photo pack

1. Approve one clean identity anchor.
2. Define assigned jobs, such as professional portrait, founder/environmental portrait, social lifestyle, travel, fitness, or editorial image.
3. Hold facial identity and retouching limits fixed while varying environment, pose, wardrobe, or lens one at a time.
4. Review every image independently. A consistent average does not excuse one different face.
5. Deliver a contact summary with job, ratio, and any rejected identity drift.

## Multi-angle person/subject reshoot

Use a fixed source and assign deliberate camera roles: overhead, fisheye, low hero, Dutch, macro detail, or ground-level. Preserve identity, outfit, environment logic, and light family. Treat views that reveal unseen anatomy or geometry as creative reconstructions, not documentary coverage.

## Fashion virtual try-on

Assign reference roles explicitly: person defines identity/body/pose; garment defines silhouette, cut, material, color, pattern, logo, and closures.

1. Generate a neutral fit proof before a styled campaign image.
2. Check garment boundaries, sleeve/hem length, drape, occlusion, hand interaction, logo/pattern continuity, and body integrity.
3. Add lifestyle context only after fit fidelity passes.
4. If exact fit or purchase decisions matter, disclose that the output is illustrative rather than a measurement-accurate fitting simulation.

## Lifestyle product try-on

Classify the product interaction: worn, held, applied, carried, or placed nearby. Build a believable use pose and environment. Product visibility cannot override natural grip/contact. Create the product-person composite first, approve it, then optionally make a less polished UGC-style context variant.

## Action figure and collectible package

Separate the transformation into identity, toy material/articulation, costume theme, accessories, and package layout. Preserve recognizable facial markers while intentionally changing proportions and surface. Inspect package copy separately; generated package text is not approved brand artwork by default.

## Chibi/sticker diary collage

Keep the source person and scene photographic. Add 5-8 assigned mini-character actions derived from the actual setting, with unique poses and protected face/body regions. Use a shared outline/shadow/palette system and limit captions to short exact strings. Reject clutter, repeated clones, changed clothing, or stickers covering the authority subject.

## Couple or multi-person grid

Create a ledger for each person and label every cell's pose, wardrobe change, and framing. Review both identities in every panel. Keep the grid geometry and backgrounds consistent; do not let costume variation swap or merge faces.

## Personal color/editorial analysis board

Use a clear, neutrally lit portrait and state that model-based color analysis is an aesthetic suggestion, not calibrated colorimetry. Build the board with portrait, proposed palette, neutrals, avoid/contrast examples, makeup/hair/jewelry suggestions, and capsule looks. Keep labels concise and verify them in text outside the generated image. Do not present inferred undertone or exact swatches as measured fact.

## Selfie or scene-composite effect

Preserve the user's source identity, clothing, phone/hand logic, and camera perspective. Build a plausible shared light and occlusion relationship between the user and the introduced scene/person. For public-figure or fictional-franchise requests, avoid claims that the image is authentic documentary evidence and respect the active content policy.
