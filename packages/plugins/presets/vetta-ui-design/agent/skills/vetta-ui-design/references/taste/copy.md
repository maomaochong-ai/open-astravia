# Copy

Words are part of the design. A strong layout with stock copy still reads as
generated; specific copy lifts an average layout. Write in the language the user
writes to you in — the rules below apply to every language, with Chinese
equivalents where the defaults differ.

## Be specific

Imitate the *kind* of specificity real products use — named audiences, named
inputs, concrete numbers the user gave, a verb that is a verb — never invent
facts about the product.

- *"Resend is the email API for developers."* — names the form factor and the audience.
- *"Open the trace, find the span, fix the regression."* — three concrete verbs.
- *"Read anything that emits lines. Files, pipes, sockets, kubectl logs."* — names the inputs.
- *"每天 30 秒，记下今天吃了什么。"* — a duration and a concrete action.

If the brief gives nothing to be specific with, use what the product obviously
does, and keep headlines short rather than grand.

exclamation marks in product UI.

## Interface copy

- **Buttons say the action:** "Save changes", "Send invitation", "创建项目" —
  not "OK", "Submit", "确定" when a specific verb exists.
- **Labels describe:** "Work email" over "Email"; "收货地址" over "地址".
- **Links stand alone:** "View pricing" over "Click here" / "点击这里".
- **One word per concept:** pick "Delete" or "Remove", "登录" or "登入", and keep it.
- **Errors:** what happened → why, if known → what to do. "That card was
  declined. Try another card or contact your bank." No "Oops!" / "出错啦~".
- **Empty states:** name what is empty, say what it is for, offer one action.
  "还没有项目 · 项目用来归拢任务和成员 · 新建项目".
- **Clickable text is one line.** Shorten the label before letting a button,
  tab or nav item wrap.

## Sample content inside screens

Realistic, audience-specific and internally consistent: names that fit the
market, prices in the right currency and magnitude, dates that line up, totals
that add up. It is sample data, so it may be invented — but a product claim
("用户增长 300 %") is not sample data; see `anti-patterns.md` § Invented proof.
