<Takeaways>
  <li>A team is a set of standing members, not a throwaway helper</li>
  <li>You talk to the owner; it decides who does what</li>
  <li>Members share results, never each other's execution history</li>
</Takeaways>

An ordinary session has one Agent: it plans, builds, and checks its own work. Once a task needs a design step, an implementation step, and someone to attack the result, that single Agent is both author and reviewer — and it rarely finds its own blind spots.

An **agent team** splits the work across several members. Each member is an independent Agent with its own responsibility, its own capability switches, and its own private conversation. One of them is the **owner** (the leader): your single point of contact, responsible for breaking the goal down, delegating each step to the member that fits it, accepting or reworking what comes back, and standing behind the final delivery.

## A team member is not a subagent

Vetta has two kinds of "multi-agent", and the boundary between them is explicit:

| | Team member | Subagent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lifetime | Standing. Persists across turns and sessions with its own history | Temporary. Created by one Agent for one job, then discarded |
| Visibility | Listed in the team roster; you can open its own session | A private helper of its creator; never in the roster |
| Ownership | Can own a team task and speak publicly as a member | Cannot own team work or publish as a member |
| Tooling | Team collaboration tools: delegate, wait, publish | Scheduled entirely by its creator |

So a team member cannot reach for subagent tooling to drive another member: those tools are removed from a team member's runtime, which keeps ownership of team work from being handed off behind the scenes.

## When a team is worth it

<Fork>
  <ForkYes title="Assemble a team">
    <li>The work has real phases: design, then build, then review.</li>
    <li>You need an independent pass at finding defects, not self-assessment.</li>
    <li>Several angles on the same material have to be merged into one deliverable.</li>
  </ForkYes>
  <ForkNo title="An ordinary session is cheaper">
    <li>The goal is single and finishes in a turn or two.</li>
    <li>You want to watch and steer the details yourself.</li>
    <li>Nothing in the task can run in parallel or needs a handoff.</li>
  </ForkNo>
</Fork>

## What you get by default

The entry point is **Agents** in the sidebar. Teams sit at the top, the agent library below — a team is assembled from agents, and one agent can serve in several teams at once.

A fresh install ships eight agents, one per role blueprint:

| Agent | Role | What it owns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Master | Owner | Breaks down the goal, delegates, accepts or reworks results, delivers |
| Researcher | Research | Collects facts, documentation, prior art, and market signals, and verifies them |
| Architect | Design | Produces the architecture, the interface contracts, or a document or PRD outline |
| Executor | Production | Produces the core asset: code, a substantive draft, or a worked analysis |
| Auditor | Audit | Red-teams the work for correctness, safety, edge cases, and unsupported claims |
| Optimizer | Refinement | Improves finished work: performance, maintainability, channel-specific voice |
| Synthesizer | Packaging | Merges several members' results into one consistent report or bundle |
| Translator | Conversion | Localizes across languages and restates technical detail in business language |

Plus four preset teams, each a fixed pipeline of one Master and three specialists:

| Team | Roster | Pipeline |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dev Team | Master · Architect · Executor · Auditor | Design and contracts first, then implementation with self-verification, then review; blocking findings go back to the executor or the design |
| Deep Research | Master · Researcher · Auditor · Synthesizer | Split the question into angles gathered in parallel, strip unsupported claims, then assemble a structured report |
| Growth & Content | Master · Researcher · Executor · Optimizer | Set the angle and evidence, write one master draft, then derive per-channel variants |
| Biz Strategy | Master · Architect · Auditor · Synthesizer | Build the PRD and business model, hunt for fatal flaws, then package a presentable plan |

None of those pipelines is hardcoded. Each lives in the **team assignment** written on that team's Master (see "Configure a team" below), so changing the workflow means editing a paragraph of text, not waiting for a release.

The preset content is materialized into ordinary team files on first launch. From then on it behaves exactly like anything you create: rename it, rewrite responsibilities, add or remove members, or delete the team outright — a deleted team is never re-injected.
