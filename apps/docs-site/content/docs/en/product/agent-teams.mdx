<Takeaways>
  <li>A team is a set of standing members, not a throwaway helper</li>
  <li>You talk to the owner; it decides who does what</li>
  <li>Members share results, never each other's execution history</li>
</Takeaways>

An ordinary session has one Agent: it plans, builds, and checks its own work. Once a task needs a design step, an implementation step, and someone to attack the result, that single Agent is both author and reviewer — and it rarely finds its own blind spots.

An **agent team** splits the work across several members. Each member is an independent Agent with its own responsibility, its own capability switches, and its own private conversation. One of them is the **owner** (the leader): your single point of contact, responsible for breaking the goal down, delegating each step to the member that fits it, accepting or reworking what comes back, and standing behind the final delivery.

## A team member is not a subagent

Vetta has two kinds of "multi-agent", and the boundary between them is explicit:

| | Team member | Subagent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lifetime | Standing. Persists across turns and sessions with its own history | Temporary. Created by one Agent for one job, then discarded |
| Visibility | Listed in the team roster; you can open its own session | A private helper of its creator; never in the roster |
| Ownership | Can own a team task and speak publicly as a member | Cannot own team work or publish as a member |
| Tooling | Team collaboration tools: delegate, wait, publish | Scheduled entirely by its creator |

So a team member cannot reach for subagent tooling to drive another member: those tools are removed from a team member's runtime, which keeps ownership of team work from being handed off behind the scenes.

## When a team is worth it

<Fork>
  <ForkYes title="Assemble a team">
    <li>The work has real phases: design, then build, then review.</li>
    <li>You need an independent pass at finding defects, not self-assessment.</li>
    <li>Several angles on the same material have to be merged into one deliverable.</li>
  </ForkYes>
  <ForkNo title="An ordinary session is cheaper">
    <li>The goal is single and finishes in a turn or two.</li>
    <li>You want to watch and steer the details yourself.</li>
    <li>Nothing in the task can run in parallel or needs a handoff.</li>
  </ForkNo>
</Fork>

## What you get by default

The entry point is **Agents** in the sidebar. Teams sit at the top, the agent library below — a team is assembled from agents, and one agent can serve in several teams at once.

  <figcaption>A team card shows its member avatars and headcount; selecting it offers to start a session or open team settings. An agent card opens that agent's profile.</figcaption>

On first launch the built-in **Preset Agents** extension lays down five general-purpose personas:

| Agent | Role | What it owns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Master | Owner | Breaks down the goal, delegates, accepts or reworks results, delivers |
| Developer | Production | Produces the core asset: code, a substantive draft, or a worked analysis |
| Researcher | Research | Collects facts, documentation, prior art, and market signals, and verifies them |
| Auditor | Audit | Red-teams the work for correctness, safety, edge cases, and unsupported claims |
| Business | Business | Turns a goal into requirements, scope, and a business model, with its assumptions named |

Plus two preset teams, both led by the Master:

| Team | Roster | Pipeline |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dev Team | Master · Developer · Researcher · Auditor | Establish the evidence, implement and self-verify, then review; blocking findings go back to the implementation or the plan |
| Planning Team | Master · Researcher · Auditor · Business | Research first, turn it into requirements and a business model, and ship only once the audit finds no fatal flaw |

These personas and teams are maintained by the extension, not by the host, which ships no presets of its own: the Agents page labels where each came from, they cannot be deleted, and disabling the extension puts them away together. Anything you create yourself is untouched.

None of those pipelines is hardcoded. Each lives in the **team assignment** written on that team's Master (see "Configure a team" below), so changing the workflow means editing a paragraph of text, not waiting for a release.

The preset content is materialized into ordinary team files on first launch. From then on it behaves exactly like anything you create: rename it, rewrite responsibilities, add or remove members, or delete the team outright — a deleted team is never re-injected.

## Run a task with a team

<Steps>
  <Step>
    ### Pick the team

    On the **new session** page, use the selector above the composer to **pick a team or a single agent**; you can also select a team card on the Agents page and start a session from it. Choosing a single agent is still an ordinary session with a different persona — only a team enters team collaboration.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Settle the workspace

    With no project selected, the session gets a **new workspace** of its own. Select a project and it works in that project directory instead. The workspace is fixed when the session is created, the coordination runtime and every member share that one directory, and later changes to your project list never redirect it.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Describe the task and send

    State the goal; with no member addressed, **the owner takes it**. To name a specific member, use `@` at the bottom left of the composer. Model, reasoning level, and execution mode belong to this team session, and each member captures the effective settings as its task is queued.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Reading and steering the timeline

| Element | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Roster bar | This session's members, the owner marked with a crown. Click one to open **its own session** and read that member's full execution history |
| Member card and state | Waiting, thinking, calling a tool, completed, or failed — the current state of that member's task |
| A `team_delegate_task` step | The owner handed a bounded objective to a member; the task is queued at that moment |
| Gear icon | Team settings: members, owner, assignments, and capabilities |

You can send another message mid-flight to add a requirement, and `@` it at someone specific. Drafts, attachments, input history, and model settings are isolated per team session, so one team can run several independent sessions at once; each appears in the sidebar conversation list with its members' stacked avatars.

## How it works: delegate, wait, accept

Team collaboration is not one prompt fanned out to several models. It is a set of **durable tasks** moving between members. The owner holds a small set of team tools, and every step you see in the timeline is one of these calls:

| Tool | What it does | Who may call it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `team_delegate_task` | Hands a bounded objective to a member and returns a task id **immediately**, without waiting for completion | Owner only |
| `team_wait_tasks` | Waits for up to eight tasks to change state, with a timeout | Parties to the task |
| `team_get_task` | Reads a task's current state, waiting reason, and published result | Parties to the task |
| `team_continue_task` / `team_retry_task` | Resumes or retries an interrupted task in the member's existing conversation, keeping the same task id | Owner or the assigned member |
| `team_cancel_task` | Cancels a task assigned to someone else | Owner only |
| `team_send_message` | Publishes to named members: `inform` just tells them, `question` asks for a reply | Every member |
| `team_list_members` | Reads the roster: responsibilities, availability, effective capabilities | Every member |
| `team_read_shared_history` | Reads the policy-allowed public history verbatim | Every member |

Three hard constraints sit behind those tools, and they explain most of what you see on screen.

### Dispatch is asynchronous, so work runs in parallel

`team_delegate_task` returns as soon as the task is **admitted**, so the owner can send out independent tasks first and wait for them together. Each member owns its own execution lane: different members genuinely run in parallel, while one member's tasks run in order.

That is also why waiting is bounded. A `team_wait_tasks` timeout only means "nothing changed in that window" — it is **not a failure and never cancels the work**. Completion arrives as a notification that wakes the initiator, so the owner does not have to burn turns polling.

Circular waits are rejected outright: if A is waiting on B, B cannot turn around and wait on A, since both would stall forever.

### Ownership is never handed off quietly

The default orchestration policy is leader-delegates: only the owner delegates and cancels; a member can only push forward the task it holds. The role prompts carry the same discipline — when information is missing, ask or report back to the owner rather than passing the assignment to someone else. Together with subagent tooling being removed from member runtimes, every task has one unambiguous holder.

### Failures are classified, not blindly retried

Task state is durable (queued, running, waiting, attention required, completed, failed, cancelled), and a task can only reach **completed once it has a published result message** — so "completed" always corresponds to something visible in the timeline.

Failures are classified by cause, which decides what can happen next:

| Cause | Handling |
| --- | --- |
| Network glitch, rate limit | Retried automatically |
| Invalid credentials, insufficient credit | Waits for the external condition; once you fix it in settings, the affected tasks are woken for retry |
| Context overflow | Needs a human decision |
| Invalid request | Not retried |

So when a member reports a failure, check which class it falls into first: retrying a task that ran out of credit will never make progress.

## How it works: results are shared, execution is not

A team session actually holds N+1 conversations: one **public coordination conversation**, plus a **private conversation** per member. The main timeline is the public one; opening a member shows you its private one.

<DataFlow>
  <div>Member private conversation<span>reasoning, tool calls, retries</span></div>
  <b>→</b>
  <div>Public coordination conversation<span>your messages + published conclusions</span></div>
  <b>→</b>
  <div>Other members' context<span>public records, projected by policy</span></div>
</DataFlow>

The default context policy projects public content only, which means:

- Members **cannot** read each other's thinking, tool transcripts, or private conversation files. What crosses the line is the conclusion a member published.
- Every member carries its own delivery cursor, so the same public record is never handed to it twice, and its own freshly published message is not projected back to it.
- As public history grows it is compacted into a shared checkpoint that the whole team references. When the summary is not enough, a member reads the summarized originals through `team_read_shared_history`, page by page. Whatever comes back is treated as **quoted conversation data**, never as instructions to follow.

That boundary is deliberate: each member reasons from a clean context instead of being flooded with four full execution logs. The cost is the obvious one — **what was never published does not reach teammates**. So when a finding should shape later steps, make it a published conclusion rather than leaving it inside one member's intermediate work.

### What the whole team can see

| Content | Visible to |
| --- | --- |
| Messages you send the team | Everyone |
| Conclusions a member publishes | Everyone |
| A member's **responsibility in this team** (one line of its assignment) | The shared roster, which is how the owner decides who gets what |
| A member's **additional team instructions** | That member only |
| A member's reasoning, tool calls, private history | That member only — though you can open its session and read it |

Event ids are also derived from stable fields such as the session, request, and member, so resubmitting the same request produces neither a duplicate public message nor duplicate execution. That is why a reconnect does not double the timeline.

## Configure a team: assignments, capabilities, owner

There are two layers to adjust: **the agent itself** (open its profile on the Agents page) and **how it behaves inside one team** (edit it in team settings).

<MediaFrame>
  <img src="/images/product/agent-profile.webp" alt="The Vetta agent profile drawer with basics, system prompt, and abilities tabs" width="1920" height="1215" />
  <figcaption>A profile has three parts: basics (avatar, name, responsibilities), system prompt, and abilities. Ability switches affect this agent only — they never install or uninstall anything globally.</figcaption>
</MediaFrame>

### The agent profile

| Field | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Responsibilities | One line that enters the shared roster and drives how the owner delegates |
| System prompt | Overrides the role blueprint's own prompt; leave it empty to keep the blueprint default |
| Abilities | The skills, scenes, MCP servers, and plugins this agent may use. It inherits **every** globally available capability until you touch a switch, which materializes a custom set |

When an agent is referenced by several teams, saving first lists the affected teams for confirmation; after that, those teams use the new configuration **from their next turn**.

### Team settings: members and assignments

The gear icon opens team settings, where you add or remove members, change the owner, and write a **team assignment** for each member:

| Assignment field | What it does | Who sees it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Responsibility in this team | Overrides the profile's responsibilities, for this team only | The shared roster |
| Additional team instructions | Team-specific direction appended to its persona | That member only |

An assignment is an **increment**: an empty field falls back to the agent profile, and nothing here affects the same agent in another team. It is appended after the persona and the role's collaboration rules rather than replacing them — so there is no need to restate rules like "report back to the owner".

The preset teams' fixed pipelines are built exactly this way: the workflow lives in the Master's additional instructions. Changing the pipeline means editing that paragraph.

Adding a member also asks how it binds:

- **Follows the agent library** (reference): edits to the profile flow into this team. This is the default.
- **This team only** (copy): a private copy of the profile, independent from the library version afterwards.

### When a change takes effect

A member runtime is rebuilt based on the profile revision plus a fingerprint of its assignment: edit either one and it applies **from the next turn**, with history preserved. Editing unrelated fields such as the team name or description never disturbs a member session in flight.

## Sessions, workspaces, and files

### One team, many sessions

A team is configuration; a session is one piece of work. The same team can run several sessions at once, each with its own workspace, drafts, attachments, model settings, and history.

The workspace is fixed when the session is created:

| Choice at creation | Working directory |
| --- | --- |
| No project selected | A new directory owned by this session, under `VETTA_HOME/agent-teams/session-workspaces/` |
| A project selected | That project's directory |

The coordination runtime and every member share that one directory, so files a member writes are visible to the others — **files are their real shared workbench**, while messages carry only conclusions.

Team sessions use the same underlying storage format as ordinary conversations, but their ownership is recorded explicitly: they never leak into the ordinary conversation list or search results, and appear in the sidebar as team sessions. For a team, the activity panel exposes only the tabs with unambiguous semantics — **files** and **browser**.

### A team is a set of files

Teams and agents live under `VETTA_HOME/agent-teams/` (`~/.vetta` by default in production):

Structured metadata — capability selections, member index, policy references — stays in the `*.json` files, while long text such as responsibilities, system prompts, and member assignments lives in separate Markdown files you can diff, version, and copy between teams. A directory name combines a readable name with an immutable id digest, so renaming a team never moves its directory.

Writes use a revision check plus atomic replacement: when the same configuration is edited in two places, the later save fails instead of silently overwriting.

## Getting good results

- **Put the acceptance bar in your first message.** The owner accepts against the goal you gave it; "build this and send it over" and "build this, have the Auditor review it, and deliver only with no blocking findings" produce very different pipelines.
- **Publish the conclusions that matter.** Teammates only see published content; a judgement buried in one member's intermediate work does not travel.
- **Change behaviour through the assignment first.** It affects this team only, and leaves the same agent untouched everywhere else.
- **More members is not better.** Each one adds model cost and a handoff; the preset teams are four people for a reason.
- **Skip the team for simple work.** If one or two turns would finish it, an ordinary session or a single agent is faster and cheaper.

## Troubleshooting

- **A task sits in "waiting"**: waiting is not failure. Check whether it is blocked on an external condition such as expired credentials or exhausted credit — fix that and the affected tasks are woken for retry. Otherwise it is simply queued or still running.
- **A member keeps drifting off target**: edit its assignment in **this team** (responsibility plus additional instructions) rather than its agent profile, which would affect every team referencing it.
- **A configuration change seems to do nothing**: profile and assignment edits apply from the **next turn**; a turn already in flight is unaffected.
- **You need to add a requirement mid-flight**: just send another message in the team session, and `@` someone to address them specifically.
- **Deleting an agent**: the affected teams are listed before you confirm. It is then removed from them; if it was the owner, ownership passes to the next remaining member, and a team left with no members at all is deleted too.
- **A team session is missing from the conversation list**: team sessions appear in the sidebar under their team identity and are deliberately kept out of the ordinary list and search results.
